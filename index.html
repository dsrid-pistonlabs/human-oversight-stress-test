THE GOVERNANCE PARADOX

Escalating more decisions
can weaken the safeguard.

When the review team is already full, routing more work to humans grows the queue. The control still exists in policy. Its ability to intervene in time may not.

Approval should attach to an operating range—not just a model.

02 / THE DECISION BRIEF

Turn the result into a condition.

ILLUSTRATIVE DEPLOYMENT CONDITION

01

Prove the review works.

Measure review time on representative cases. Test whether reviewers detect errors, have enough context and can actually stop or reverse an action.

02

Name the intervention.

Assign an operations owner to queue age and demand. Give a named decision owner authority to restrict automation when the operating limit is exceeded.

03

Choose the consequence.

Hold flagged actions, narrow the automated workflow or add qualified capacity. Each has a cost. Automatically proceeding changes the control and requires a separate decision.

Capacity is necessary. It is not evidence of effective oversight.

A short queue can still conceal rubber-stamping, poor judgment or cases that should have been escalated but never were. This test measures workload feasibility, not safety or compliance.

03 / METHOD & LIMITS

A transparent model.
A deliberately narrow claim.

How the numbers work

Daily review demand = decisions × review percentage. Daily capacity = reviewers × 360 productive minutes ÷ minutes per case. End-of-day queue = max(0, previous queue + demand − capacity). Backlog tolerance = selected working days × daily capacity. A breach is the first end-of-day queue strictly above that tolerance.

The sustainable decision volume shown below is the mathematical break-even point, rounded down. It includes no resilience buffer and is not a recommended deployment limit.

What this does not model

This is a deterministic, daily, first-in-first-out approximation with constant demand, uniform review times, no absences and an empty starting queue. It does not estimate actual waiting-time distributions or deadline breaches. Bursty arrivals, complex cases, correlated failures and reviewer fatigue need empirical testing. Near 100% utilisation, this model hides the effect of variability.

Backlog divided by daily capacity is a workload indicator, not a promise about how long an individual waits. Working days exclude any calendar or overnight service assumptions. All scenarios are synthetic; no operational data is used.
